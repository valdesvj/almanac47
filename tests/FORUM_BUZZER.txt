Hi all,

I am an old fan of the Commodore 64, so I decided to see what the C47 buzzer can do from a program.

I read the sound code in the C47 firmware (src/c47/ui/tone.c and the hal/audio.c files).

I was surprised by what I found, and with the help of AI I made a little demo of Commodore 64 tricks on one voice.

THE SOUND COMMANDS (C47 firmware, master)

  TONE n    one fixed note of 250 ms, n = 0..9:
            E3 A3 B3 C#4 D4 E4 F#4 G#4 A4 C#5 (164.8 ... 554.4 Hz)
  BEEP      TONE 8, 5, 9, 8 (A4 E4 C#5 A4)
  BUZZ      Y = frequency in Hz (whole Hz, 0 = silence, max 20 kHz),
            X = length in ms (max 2000). The program waits until the note ends.
  PLAY r    plays an N x 2 matrix (Hz, ms per row). After each note a silence of
            1/8 of its length; EXIT stops it. An N x 3 matrix adds a volume (0..11)
            per note, and your volume setting is put back at the end.
  VOL n     buzzer volume 0..11; VOL# reads it; VOL^ / VOLv one step up / down.
  System flag QUIET silences everything.

ONE VOICE AGAINST THE SID

The C64's SID had 3 voices, 4 waveforms (triangle, saw, pulse, noise), an ADSR
envelope per voice, a resonant filter, ring modulation and sync. The C47 has one
buzzer, one note at a time, no envelope, no filter. So it is closer to a PC speaker
or a ZX Spectrum beeper than to a SID.

But the C64 composers used tricks that work on a beeper too, and C64FX plays three
of them (an original tune in A minor, about 15 s):

  1. Fake chords: the three notes of a chord in turn, 30 ms each, 12 rounds.
     The ear hears a chord.
  2. Vibrato: a long note cut into 25 ms pieces whose pitch goes up and down
     1.2 % along a sine (about 6.7 Hz).
  3. Plucked notes: each note cut into 5 pieces of 40 ms at volume 11 8 6 4 2,
     a crude envelope.

It uses BUZZ (no gap between notes, which the arpeggios need), local registers
only, and gives your volume back at the end (VOL# at the start, VOL IND at the end).
Note: it sets the angle mode to DEG for the vibrato's SIN.

THE FILES

  C64FX.p47 / C64FX.txt   the demo (998 bytes), XEQ "C64FX"
  C64FX_doc.txt           the listing with a comment on every step

It only works on the calculator: the PC simulator has no sound for BUZZ.
Have fun, and turn the volume down before trying it in a meeting.
